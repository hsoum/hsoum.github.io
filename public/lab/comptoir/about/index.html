Since 2021

From Lyon to Smith Street

Émile Dupont grew up just outside Lyon, where Sunday lunches ran for hours and the market set the menu. He trained in the city's old-guard kitchens before spending six years on the line at a Michelin-starred restaurant in Paris — learning the discipline of French cooking and, eventually, what he wanted to leave behind.

In 2021 he traded Paris for Brooklyn and opened Le Comptoir d'Émile on Smith Street. The idea was simple: the seasonal French cooking he grew up on, stripped of ceremony — great produce, a short menu, natural wine, and a room where regulars and first-timers sit shoulder to shoulder.

In the Kitchen

Sourced close, cooked simply

We source from local farms within 100 miles and let the week's harvest write the menu — which is why it changes every six weeks. Nothing leaves the pass with foam or tweezers; we'd rather you taste the carrot than admire it.

The open kitchen, mid-service.

How We Work

From market to table

Four steps, every week. It's why the menu never sits still — and why a carrot in October tastes nothing like the same plate in June.

  1. Walk the market

    Each week starts at the farms within 100 miles. We buy what's best that morning, not what's on a standing order.

  2. Write the menu

    The harvest sets the plates. The card stays short and is rewritten every six weeks as the season turns.

  3. Cook it simply

    Classic French technique in service of the ingredient — no foam, no tweezers, nothing to hide behind.

  4. Pour the natural wine

    Julien pairs each dish from an all-natural cellar, glass or bottle, familiar or pleasantly strange.
